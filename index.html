What is WCAG 2.2?

The Web Content Accessibility Guidelines are the W3C's international standard for accessible websites and apps. Version 2.2 became a W3C Recommendation in October 2023. Everything sits under four principles.

Who each principle helps most

P

Perceivable

People can perceive the content: text alternatives, captions, enough contrast.

Helps most: Eye and Ear

O

Operable

People can use it: keyboard, no time pressure, nothing that triggers seizures.

Helps most: Motor

U

Understandable

Content and behaviour are readable and predictable, with clear errors.

Helps most: Cognition

R

Robust

It works reliably with browsers and assistive technology like screen readers.

Helps most: Motor and Cognition

Eye

Vision: blind, low-vision and colour-blind people.

Ear

Hearing: Deaf and hard-of-hearing people.

Motor

Movement: tremors, limited dexterity, keyboard, switch and voice users.

Cognition

Thinking: memory, attention, learning and reading differences.

Every principle helps everyone in some way. The arrows show who each one helps most.

Three levels, each building on the last

Every success criterion has a conformance level. AA means every A criterion plus every AA criterion; AAA includes all three.

A

The essentials

The minimum. If these fail, some people cannot use the site at all.

AA

The standard target

What most laws, clients and organisations require. Aim for this level in your beta.

AAA

The highest bar

Stricter, and not always possible across a whole site. Apply it where it matters most.

Each criterion below shows a version that fails and one that passes. Open “How to test” for details.

A

Level A: the essentials

The minimum. If these fail, some people cannot use the site at all.

  • 1.1.1 Non-text content
  • 1.2.2 Captions (prerecorded)
  • 1.3.1 Info and relationships
  • 1.4.1 Use of colour
  • 2.1.1 Keyboard
  • 2.2.2 Pause, stop, hide
  • 2.3.1 Three flashes or below
  • 2.4.2 Page titled
1.1.1Level A

Non-text content

Images, icons and visuals have a text alternative that says what they show.

How to test

For a 3D canvas, give it role="img" and an aria-label, or put a text description next to it. Purely decorative visuals should be hidden from assistive technology. Test: turn on VoiceOver or NVDA and listen.

1.2.2Level A

Captions (prerecorded)

Recorded video with sound has synchronised captions.

How to test

Auto-generated captions are a starting point, not a pass. Edit them for accuracy, speaker names and meaningful sounds. Silent background loops don't need captions.

1.3.1Level A

Info and relationships

Structure lives in the code, not only in how it looks.

How to test

The same applies to lists, tables and form labels: use the real element so the relationship is in the code. Test: a headings-outline browser extension, or a screen reader's headings list.

1.4.1Level A

Use of colour

Colour is never the only way information is shown.

How to test

Also applies to links (underline them, not just a colour change), charts (labels or patterns, not only coloured lines) and status dots. Test: view the page in greyscale.

2.1.1Level A

Keyboard

Everything works with a keyboard alone, no mouse needed.

How to test

The quickest test there is: put the mouse away and try to use the whole site with Tab, Shift+Tab, Enter, Space, Esc and the arrow keys. Also check there's no keyboard trap (2.1.2), for example inside a 3D canvas.

2.2.2Level A

Pause, stop, hide

Anything that moves for more than 5 seconds can be paused, stopped or hidden.

How to test

Applies to carousels, auto-rotating models, marquee text, background video and looping animations that run alongside other content. Respecting prefers-reduced-motion helps too, but a visible control is what this criterion requires.

2.3.1Level A

Three flashes or below

Nothing flashes more than three times in any one second.

Flashing warning: the failing example below flashes rapidly, but only after you press its button, and only for 2 seconds. If you are sensitive to flashing light, skip it.

How to test

Saturated red flashes are especially risky. Small flashing areas are lower risk, but the simplest rule for students is: no more than three flashes per second, ever. Tools like PEAT can analyse video for flash risk.

2.4.2Level A

Page titled

Every page has a clear, unique title in the browser tab.

How to test

Vite, Next and other starter templates ship with a placeholder title. Students should change it on every page or route. Single-page apps should update document.title when the view changes.

AA

Level AA: the standard target

What most laws, clients and organisations require. This is the level to aim for in your beta.

  • 1.4.3 Contrast (minimum)
  • 1.4.11 Non-text contrast
  • 1.3.4 Orientation
  • 1.4.10 Reflow
  • 1.4.13 Content on hover or focus
  • 2.4.7 Focus visible
  • 2.5.7 Dragging movements
  • 2.5.8 Target size (minimum)
1.4.3Level AA

Contrast (minimum)

Text contrast of at least 4.5 : 1, or 3 : 1 for large text (24 px+, or 19 px+ bold).

How to test

Test with the WebAIM contrast checker, or the contrast readout in Chrome DevTools' colour picker. Text over images or 3D scenes needs checking against the lightest and darkest parts behind it.

1.4.11Level AA

Non-text contrast

Buttons, inputs, icons and meaningful graphics reach 3 : 1 against what's behind them.

How to test

Covers the boundaries of controls (input borders, toggle tracks), focus indicators, icons that carry meaning, and chart lines. Decorative graphics and disabled controls are exempt.

1.3.4Level AA

Orientation

The site works in both portrait and landscape, never locked to one.

How to test

For 3D: adjust the camera's field of view or distance based on aspect ratio, so the subject stays in frame in portrait. Only lock orientation when it's essential, like a piano app.

1.4.10Level AA

Reflow

Content fits a 320 px wide view (400% zoom) without scrolling sideways.

How to test

Test: set the browser to 1280 px wide and zoom to 400%, or use DevTools at 320 px wide. Maps, data tables and 3D canvases can scroll in two directions, but the text around them must reflow.

1.4.13Level AA

Content on hover or focus

Tooltips and pop-ups can be dismissed, hovered over, and stay until you're done.

How to test

Also show the tooltip on keyboard focus, not only on mouse hover. Native title attributes fail this criterion in most browsers, so build a proper tooltip instead.

2.4.7Level AA

Focus visible

When you use the keyboard, you can always see which element has focus.

How to test

Designers often remove the outline because it looks ugly on mouse click. :focus-visible solves that: the ring shows for keyboard users only. Restyle it, never delete it.

2.5.7Level AANew in 2.2

Dragging movements

Anything done by dragging can also be done with single clicks or taps.

How to test

This is the criterion most experimental 3D sites fail. Applies to orbit controls, sliders, drag-to-reorder lists, and before/after image comparers. Dragging can stay; it just can't be the only way.

2.5.8Level AANew in 2.2

Target size (minimum)

Click and tap targets are at least 24 × 24 px, or spaced so a 24 px circle fits around each one.

How to test

Links inside a sentence are exempt. The AAA version (2.5.5) asks for 44 × 44 px, which is the size Apple and Google recommend for touch anyway.

AAA

Level AAA: the highest bar

Stricter, and not always possible across a whole site. Apply it where it matters most. Two more AAA criteria, 2.4.12 and 2.4.13, are in the New in 2.2 section.

  • 1.4.6 Contrast (enhanced)
  • 2.3.3 Animation from interactions
  • 2.5.5 Target size (enhanced)
  • 2.2.3 No timing
  • 1.4.8 Visual presentation
  • 3.1.5 Reading level
1.4.6Level AAA

Contrast (enhanced)

Text contrast of at least 7 : 1, or 4.5 : 1 for large text.

How to test

A good AAA habit that costs nothing: use near-black for body text and keep the soft greys for large headings or decoration. Same tools as 1.4.3: the WebAIM checker or DevTools.

2.3.3Level AAA

Animation from interactions

Motion triggered by scrolling or clicking can be turned off, unless it's essential.

How to test

The most relevant AAA criterion for scroll-driven 3D sites. In code: check the prefers-reduced-motion media query, then shorten or skip camera tweens and parallax. A visible "reduce motion" toggle on the page is even better.

2.5.5Level AAA

Target size (enhanced)

Click and tap targets are at least 44 × 44 px.

How to test

Compare with 2.5.8 at AA (24 px). For mobile-first student sites, 44 px is an easy default for every button and icon target, even when the visible icon is smaller.

2.2.3Level AAA

No timing

No time limits at all, except for real-time events like live auctions.

How to test

At Level A, 2.2.1 Timing Adjustable already requires that users can turn off, adjust or extend most time limits. AAA goes further: no time limits at all. Also watch out for intro sequences that auto-advance before people have read them.

1.4.8Level AAA

Visual presentation

Text blocks are easy to read: max 80 characters per line, not justified, 1.5 line spacing.

How to test

The full criterion also asks that users can choose text and background colours, and that text can be resized to 200% without horizontal scrolling. In CSS: max-width around 65ch, line-height 1.5, and no text-align: justify.

3.1.5Level AAA

Reading level

Text is understandable at lower-secondary level, or a simpler version is offered.

How to test

Students don't have to give up their critical voice; they add a plain summary on top. Tools like Hemingway Editor give a quick reading-level estimate.

2.2

New in WCAG 2.2

Nine success criteria were added in WCAG 2.2: two at A, four at AA and three at AAA. Two of them, Dragging movements (2.5.7) and Target size (2.5.8), are covered in the AA section. 4.1.1 Parsing was removed.

  • 2.4.11 Focus not obscured (minimum)
  • 2.4.12 Focus not obscured (enhanced)
  • 2.4.13 Focus appearance
  • 3.2.6 Consistent help
  • 3.3.7 Redundant entry
  • 3.3.8 Accessible authentication (minimum)
  • 3.3.9 Accessible authentication (enhanced)
2.4.11Level AANew in 2.2

Focus not obscured (minimum)

The focused element is never completely hidden by banners, sticky bars or overlays.

How to test

Fixes: CSS scroll-padding-bottom equal to the sticky bar's height, smaller banners, or banners that must be dismissed before you continue. Test by tabbing through the page with the cookie banner open.

2.4.12Level AAANew in 2.2

Focus not obscured (enhanced)

No part of the focused element is hidden, not even a corner.

How to test

The AA version allows partial overlap. AAA wants the whole element visible. The same fix works for both: scroll-padding on the page that matches the height of any sticky header or footer.

2.4.13Level AAANew in 2.2

Focus appearance

The focus indicator is big enough and contrasting enough to spot at a glance.

How to test

This criterion was a minimum (AA) in drafts, but the final 2.2 standard put it at AAA. It's still the clearest target for designing focus styles: aim for it even when you only need AA.

3.2.6Level ANew in 2.2

Consistent help

If help or contact options appear on several pages, they're in the same place each time.

How to test

Help means contact details, a contact form, a chat, or a FAQ link. This criterion doesn't require help to exist, only that it's consistent when it does. Especially helpful for people with cognitive disabilities.

3.3.7Level ANew in 2.2

Redundant entry

Don't make people re-type what they already entered in the same process.

How to test

Within a single process, information already entered must either be filled in automatically or be available to select. Exceptions: re-entering a password for security, or when the old information is no longer valid.

3.3.8Level AANew in 2.2

Accessible authentication (minimum)

Logging in never depends on remembering, transcribing or solving something, without an alternative.

How to test

Allowed at AA: CAPTCHAs that ask you to recognise objects, or to identify content you uploaded yourself. Not allowed without an alternative: remembering a password with paste blocked, transcribing distorted characters, or solving puzzles.

3.3.9Level AAANew in 2.2

Accessible authentication (enhanced)

No cognitive test at all to log in, including object or image recognition.

How to test

Most student betas won't have logins. Where they do, passkeys or email magic links meet both the AA and AAA versions of this criterion. Bot protection can run invisibly in the background instead.

Introduction to responsiveness on the web

A responsive site is one design that adapts to any screen size, input and user setting, from a 320 px phone to a wide monitor, by touch, mouse or keyboard, and at 400% zoom. It's also where several WCAG criteria meet: 1.4.10 Reflow, 1.3.4 Orientation, 2.5.8 Target size and 1.4.4 Resize text.

Six ideas to design by

Fluid, not fixed

Lay out with proportions (fractions, percentages, max-widths) instead of fixed pixel widths, so content stretches and shrinks smoothly.

Breakpoints follow content

Change the layout where your design breaks, not at “iPhone size”. Resize slowly and watch for the moment it stops working.

Mobile first

Start with the smallest screen: one column, the essentials. Then add columns and extras as space allows.

Touch is not a mouse

No hover on phones, fat fingers, and gestures that can scroll the page. Big targets (44 px), and nothing that only works on hover.

Respect the person's settings

Zoom, larger text, reduce motion, dark mode, landscape lock: the user's settings beat your layout.

Content stays the same

Rearrange and simplify, but don't hide key content from phone users. Same story, different layout.

Responsive 3D: canvas and camera

  1. Size the canvas to its container, and update it when the window resizes or the phone rotates.
  2. Reframe the camera for the aspect ratio: pull back or widen the field of view in portrait so the subject stays in shot.
  3. Cap the resolution. Limit pixel ratio to about 2 (1.5 on phones); rendering at 3× retina is slow and drains the battery.
  4. Lower the complexity on small or slow devices: fewer lights and particles, smaller textures, simpler models.
  5. Don't trap the scroll. Decide whether a swipe moves the page or the camera; never both.
  6. Have a fallback. A poster image or static version for when 3D fails or loads too slowly.

Test your beta like this

  • Browser dev tools device mode at 320, 375, 768 and 1280 px wide
  • Zoom to 200% and 400%: does everything still reflow?
  • A real phone, in portrait and landscape
  • Tab through with the keyboard at a narrow width
  • Throttle the network to Slow 4G: what appears first?
  • Turn on reduce motion and larger text in your phone's settings
